# Security-Awareness-Programme
A 12-month security awareness programme designed for Northbridge Logistics covering human related risks, employee training, phishing awareness, security metrics and continuous improvement. 
# Design a Security Awareness Programme

## Northbridge Logistics

## Project Overview

This project was developed as part of my GRC206 – Design a Security Awareness Program assignment. The aim was to design a practical 12-month security awareness programme for Northbridge Logistics, a fictional organisation with 800 employees spread across its head office, warehouses and remote sales teams.

The assignment presented a few security concerns that required attention. These included attempts at invoice fraud, two employees reusing passwords following a breach, and the responsibility of protecting regulated customer information.

While working on the assignment, I considered how employees' everyday activities can affect an organisation's security. Having security policies and technical controls in place is important, but employees also need to understand the risks associated with their responsibilities.

My approach was to develop a programme that would help employees recognise suspicious activities, handle information properly and report security concerns without hesitation.

## 1. Assessment of Human-Related Security Risks

The first stage involved identifying the major risks that could arise from employee actions or lack of awareness.

I identified three priority risks:

### Invoice Fraud and Social Engineering

Invoice fraud was a major concern because employees could receive fraudulent payment instructions or emails from individuals pretending to be legitimate suppliers or senior members of staff.

The Finance team is particularly exposed because of its involvement in payment processing and financial transactions. Employees need to understand the importance of verifying payment changes through an approved communication channel before taking action.

### Password Reuse and Account Compromise

The scenario mentioned two employees who reused passwords following a breach. This highlighted the risk of using the same password across multiple platforms.

If one account becomes compromised, attackers may attempt to use the same credentials to access other business systems. Employees therefore need awareness of password security, multifactor authentication and the importance of reporting suspected account compromise.

### Mishandling of Customer Information

Northbridge Logistics handles regulated customer information, making data protection an important part of the awareness programme.

Employees may expose information through careless sharing, unsecured devices, inappropriate access or failure to follow established procedures.

The programme therefore includes training on data classification, secure information sharing and the proper handling of customer records.

## 2. Employee Audiences and Training Needs

I did not consider it practical to give every employee exactly the same training. Different departments have different responsibilities, and their exposure to security risks may also differ.

The programme therefore identifies five main audiences:

| Employee group | Main awareness needs |
|---|---|
| Finance team | Invoice fraud, payment verification, phishing and business email compromise |
| Head office employees | Password security, phishing, information handling and secure communication |
| Warehouse employees | Physical security, device protection and safe handling of operational information |
| Remote sales employees | Secure remote working, phishing, device security and customer data protection |
| Managers and supervisors | Security responsibilities, incident escalation and encouraging early reporting |

## 3. Baseline Assessment

Before launching the programme, I proposed carrying out a baseline assessment to understand the organisation's existing security awareness level.

The assessment would consider:

- Phishing simulation results to establish how employees respond to suspicious emails.
- Password and authentication practices to identify weaknesses in account protection.
- Previous security incidents and helpdesk reports to understand recurring employee-related issues.
- Existing training records to determine the current level of awareness training completion.
- Data-handling records and reported incidents to identify possible weaknesses in protecting customer information.

The results would provide a starting point for measuring improvement throughout the 12-month programme.

## 4. Programme Planning and Objectives

After identifying the risks and employee groups, I developed three measurable objectives.

1. Reduce the phishing simulation click rate by 50% against the baseline by the end of Month 12.
2. Increase the reporting of simulated phishing messages by 40% against the baseline by the end of Month 12.
3. Achieve at least 95% completion of core security awareness training by Month 6.

These objectives were selected because they measure different aspects of the programme: employee behaviour, willingness to report suspicious activities and training participation.

### Programme Responsibilities

The programme requires support from different departments rather than depending entirely on the security team.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Chief Operating Officer | Executive sponsorship and support |
| Information Security Manager | Programme coordination, risk assessment and reporting |
| Human Resources | Training coordination and onboarding |
| IT Team | Password security, authentication and technical support |
| Finance Manager | Payment verification and invoice fraud awareness |
| Data Protection Officer or Privacy Lead | Customer information protection and data-handling awareness |
| Departmental Managers | Encouraging participation and monitoring employee engagement |

The proposed structure helps distribute responsibilities while keeping programme oversight with the security team.

## 5. Programme Delivery

The delivery approach considers the organisation's 800 employees, different working environments, small security team and limited budget.

Rather than relying on expensive training sessions, I proposed a combination of practical and accessible learning methods.

These include:

- Short online awareness modules for employees who can access the organisation's learning platform.
- Departmental briefings covering risks relevant to specific job responsibilities.
- Warehouse toolbox talks to reach employees who may not regularly use office computers.
- Onboarding sessions to introduce new employees to security policies and reporting procedures.
- Controlled phishing simulations to help employees recognise suspicious messages.

An important part of the programme is the reporting process. Employees should have a clear and accessible way to report suspicious emails or possible security incidents.

I also recommended a no-blame approach to phishing simulations. The purpose should be to identify knowledge gaps and improve awareness, not to embarrass employees who make mistakes.

## 6. Measuring Programme Effectiveness

One thing I considered important was that the organisation should not measure success only by the number of employees who complete training.

The programme should also assess whether employees are changing their behaviour.

I proposed the following metrics:

| Metric | How it will be measured | Evidence source | Review frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Training completion rate | Employees who complete training ÷ employees assigned training × 100 | Training records | Monthly |
| Phishing click rate | Employees who click simulated phishing links ÷ employees who receive the simulation × 100 | Phishing simulation reports | After each simulation |
| Phishing reporting rate | Employees who report simulated phishing messages ÷ employees who receive the simulation × 100 | Reporting platform or simulation records | After each simulation |
| Security incident trend | Compare the number and type of relevant incidents over time | Incident register | Monthly |
| Reporting time | Average time between an employee receiving or identifying a suspicious message and reporting it | Incident and reporting records | Monthly |

The Information Security Manager would coordinate the reporting process, with relevant data supplied by IT, Human Resources and departmental managers.

The results would be presented to management to highlight progress, identify weaknesses and determine where additional awareness activities are required.

The figures would also need to be interpreted carefully. For example, an increase in reported incidents may indicate that employees are becoming more willing to report suspicious activities rather than that security incidents are necessarily increasing.

## 7. Continuous Improvement

The final stage focuses on reviewing the programme and making improvements throughout the year.

I proposed quarterly reviews involving the Information Security Manager, IT, Human Resources, relevant departmental managers and other stakeholders where necessary.

The reviews would consider:

- Training completion records.
- Phishing simulation results.
- Reported security incidents.
- Employee feedback.
- Changes in business operations and security threats.
- Progress against the programme objectives.

Feedback should be collected from employees across different departments and shifts so that the programme reflects their actual working experiences.

I also included recognition as a way of encouraging positive security behaviour. Examples include recognising employees who report suspicious messages and acknowledging departments that demonstrate consistent participation in awareness activities.

The programme should be updated whenever there is a significant security incident, a new threat, a change in business processes or feedback showing that employees need additional guidance.

## 8. Twelve-Month Programme Calendar

The programme calendar provides a practical schedule for implementing the proposed activities.

| Month | Planned activity |
|---|---|
| Month 1 | Conduct baseline assessment and identify existing awareness gaps |
| Month 2 | Launch the programme and introduce general security awareness |
| Month 3 | Deliver phishing and social engineering awareness training; conduct first quarterly review |
| Month 4 | Deliver invoice fraud and payment verification awareness |
| Month 5 | Conduct password security and multifactor authentication awareness |
| Month 6 | Review training completion and conduct the second quarterly review |
| Month 7 | Deliver customer information protection and data-handling training |
| Month 8 | Conduct secure remote working and device security awareness |
| Month 9 | Run another phishing simulation and conduct the third quarterly review |
| Month 10 | Deliver departmental refresher sessions based on identified weaknesses |
| Month 11 | Collect employee feedback and review outstanding awareness gaps |
| Month 12 | Conduct final programme evaluation, compare results with the baseline and prepare management recommendations |

The calendar is designed to spread activities across the year instead of overwhelming employees with too much training at once.

## 9. Key Lessons and Personal Reflection

One of the main challenges I considered while developing this programme was how to reach 800 employees across different locations with a small security team and limited budget. I realised that a single training approach would not be effective for everyone because employees have different responsibilities and exposure to security risks. I therefore proposed a combination of online training, departmental briefings and practical awareness sessions. Another important consideration was how to measure whether employees were actually becoming more security-conscious. Training attendance alone would not provide enough evidence. Monitoring phishing behaviour, reporting patterns and employee feedback would give the organisation a better understanding of progress. This assignment helped me appreciate that an effective security awareness programme requires planning, management support, employee participation and continuous improvement.
